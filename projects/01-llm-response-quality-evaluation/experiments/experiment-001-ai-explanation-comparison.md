Experiment 001: AI Explanation Comparison

1. Objective

Compare two large language models based on their ability to explain Artificial Intelligence to a 10-year-old.

The evaluation focuses on instruction following, accuracy, relevance, completeness and clarity.

2. Models Evaluated

- Claude Haiku 4.5
- Gemini 3 Flash

3. Test Prompt

Explain what artificial intelligence is to a 10-year-old.

Requirements:

1. Use exactly three sentences.
2. Include one everyday example.
3. Use simple English.
4. Do not use technical jargon.

4. Model Responses

Claude Haiku 4.5

Artificial intelligence is when computers learn to do things by looking at lots of examples, kind of like how you learn by practice. For example, when you play a video game and the computer player gets better at playing against you, that's artificial intelligence making it smarter. It helps people do things faster and solve problems in ways that would take humans much longer to figure out.

Gemini 3 Flash

Artificial intelligence is when computers learn how to think and make smart decisions just like a person. For example, it is what suggests a new movie for you to watch based on what you already liked. This helps machines understand what we need to make our daily lives easier.

5. Evaluation Criteria

Scores use a 1–5 scale.

Criterion| Claude| Gemini
Instruction following| 5| 5
Accuracy| 3| 2
Relevance| 5| 5
Completeness| 4| 4
Clarity| 5| 5
Original total| 22/25| 21/25

6. Score Calibration Review

Following a review of the accuracy scores, the evaluator considered whether the original ratings were too severe.

Criterion| Claude| Gemini
Instruction following| 5| 5
Accuracy| 4| 3
Relevance| 5| 5
Completeness| 4| 4
Clarity| 5| 5
Calibrated total| 23/25| 22/25

These calibrated scores are proposed after review, rather than the evaluator's original independent scores.

7. Final Selection

Preferred model: Claude Haiku 4.5

Claude provides a more realistic description of AI learning. Gemini's comparison of AI thinking just like a human is misleading.

Both responses follow the instructions and provide relevant examples, but Claude has a slight advantage in factual accuracy.

8. Key Learning

- Following instructions does not automatically mean a response is factually accurate.
- Simple explanations should not introduce misleading comparisons.
- Evaluators should score the entire response fairly.
- Score calibration helps improve consistency.
- A preference decision should be supported by specific evidence.
